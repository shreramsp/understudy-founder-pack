# Riskiest assumptions — the ranked board

> **What this is** — every load-bearing assumption in the pack, ranked, with current evidence, the cheapest decisive test, its cost, and its status.
> **Why it exists** — a pack of sixty artifacts can make an unevidenced claim look settled simply by repeating it. This board is where each claim is stated once, with its evidence and its kill condition, so the run can be judged on what it actually knows.
> **How to read it** — rows 1–3 are the company. A skeptic should check that every `kills-company` row has a test that costs less than the round being raised.

## What this board says about the company

**Every row was `untested` when this board was written; R3's hours half is now falsified.** `financials/pricing.md` §4 shows the fee priced at 99–466% of displaced hours under one consistent method, so R3 reads **partially invalidated** — only the library half remains open, and E3's split sample is what tests it. Everything else is the honest state of a pre-build company, and stating it in a single column is more useful than distributing the same fact across sixty artifacts.

**Four rows kill the company and three of them cost under $2,000 to test.** R3, R4 and R5 need no product at all — a price conversation, three phone calls about membership rules, and an afternoon attacking our own envelope schema. **They should not be waiting behind a ten-week build**, and the fact that the 90-day motion in `strategy/gtm.md` runs E3 and E4 in its first thirty days is the plan working correctly.

**R1 is the only expensive one, and its true price is the round — ~$435k, not the $1,600 contractor line.** It needs the harness, which needs the pipeline.

**Note the ordering the table does not show.** This board ranks by *consequence*; resolution order is close to the inverse. **R3 and R4 gate ~79% of year-1 revenue and every channel respectively, cost roughly nothing, and return in month 2–3 — nine months before R1 returns anything.** A reader taking the rank order as a work order would build for a year before learning the price has no basis.

**R2 is where the pack is most exposed to its own reasoning.** The RPA differentiation argument has been corrected three times under critique — coordinates, then interface-vs-goal absolutes, then the fallback question. Each correction narrowed it. **A claim that has needed three corrections before anyone measured it should be measured before it is sold**, which is why E2 is ranked second rather than filed under nice-to-have.

**One structural note the earlier version missed.** E9 and E10 were cited here as tests and appeared on no board row — a dangling reference in the file whose entire job is to connect assumptions to the experiments that retire them. Both are now rows.

**Recommended next 3:** (1) run E3, E4 and E5 immediately — they are cheap, they need no code, and any one coming back negative reshapes the company; (2) build the harness before the clusterer so E1 produces evidence rather than opinion; (3) re-rank this board after the first five results, since a board written before any data is a hypothesis about what matters.
