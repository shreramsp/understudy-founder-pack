# Mission, vision, values

> **What this is** — what the company does and for whom (true today), the world if it succeeds, the trade-offs it will actually make, and why it exists at all.
> **Why it exists** — positioning is defined against competitors and changes when they do; mission is independent of them and survives their disappearance. This file is what remains if Ravenna, Atomicwork and ServiceNow all vanished tomorrow.
> **How to read it** — the values are the operative section, because each is stated as a trade-off with a cost rather than as a virtue. A skeptic should test whether each one would ever actually bind.
> **Depends on / feeds** — independent of [../strategy/positioning.md](../strategy/positioning.md) by design; feeds [founder_story.md](founder_story.md), `startup-website` (about page).

## Mission — true today

**Make the knowledge of how to run an IT environment something a business owns, rather than something three people happen to remember.**

## Vision — ten years out

Environment knowledge stops being tacit. An MSP takes on a client and the desk already knows how that client's stack behaves, because it watched someone competent do the work once and kept the parts that mattered. **MSPs run many more clients per engineer**, not because engineers were replaced but because nobody re-derives what a colleague already worked out.

The broader shift, if it holds: software that learns its environment by observation becomes the default expectation rather than a differentiating feature — the way that "it integrates with your identity provider" stopped being a selling point and became table stakes.

## Values — each stated as a trade-off we will actually make

**1. The person being recorded gets the credit.**
*The trade-off:* attribution, correction rights and rejection-without-justification are engineering cost, and they slow the library down — an engineer can veto a skill we believe is correct, and we will lose that argument. We pay it because a capture programme that feels like surveillance degrades quietly, and no dashboard shows it happening.

**2. State the limit before someone finds it.**
*The trade-off:* our own deck says the first environment costs more than doing nothing, that where a real API exists we are the wrong tool, and that we have no traction. Each of those costs us a percentage of readers. We pay it because the alternative is a design partner's timesheet contradicting our invoice, which costs us the reference.

**3. Build the measurement before the thing being measured.**
*The trade-off:* the evaluation harness ships before the clusterer, which looks like a quarter spent on something undemoable. We pay it because a system with no benchmark grades its own homework, and this one has no benchmark to inherit.

**4. Refuse the action rather than guess it.**
*The trade-off:* escalating when a situation leaves the envelope means a lower automation rate and a worse-looking number. We pay it because IT actions are frequently irreversible, and a confident wrong action costs more trust than ten honest refusals.

**5. Do not claim the autonomy we cannot evidence.**
*The trade-off:* the category's excitement is about unattended resolution, and declining to promise it means being less exciting than competitors on the axis buyers currently ask about. We pay it because long-horizon completion is 20.6% and the gap between demo and production is where this category will lose its credibility.

## Why we exist

Because the most valuable thing in a managed-services business walks out of the building every evening, and occasionally does not come back. Everything else — the tooling, the tickets, the documentation nobody keeps current — is an attempt to write that down, and it fails for a structural reason: **writing it down is a separate act from doing it, so it competes with billable work and loses.**

Understudy exists because the two acts can now be the same act.
